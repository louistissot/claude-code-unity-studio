# Playtest plan: <build> (<date>)

A playtest with people who did not make the game. The human play report shows what the developer feels; a playtest
shows what a new player understands. Plan, run, analyse, review, and do it again.

## 1. Objectives

What do we want to learn? Two to four questions, for example:

1. <Do new players find the way out of the start area without help?>
2. <Do players understand when an enemy hears them?>

## 2. Players and setup

- <number> players who did not play the game before, <the kind of player>.
- The build: <release tag>, on <machines>. The settings: <quality, controls>.
- The session: <minutes> of play, then <minutes> of questions.

## 3. Observation

- Ask the players to say what they think while they play (think-aloud), or record the screen and the voice, and ask
  later. Think-aloud can change how a person plays: mix both methods.
- The observer writes the time and what happens at each moment of the objectives. The observer does not help, except
  when a player is stuck for more than <minutes>.

## 4. Telemetry

The events and values that answer the objectives, for example the path, the deaths and their causes, the time to each
goal, the use of each item. Keep the list short: each event costs time in the analysis.

## 5. Questions after the play

1. <What was the goal of the game? (open question)>
2. <What was the hardest moment?>
3. <Rate from 1 to 5: ...>
