# The originality gate: challenge record for <title> (<date>)

Keep this record as `concept-challenge.md` next to the concept document. The challenger asks each question. The human
answers in the human's own words. The challenger gives a verdict with a reason. The concept gate needs **all parts
accepted** ([docs/concept-phase.md](../docs/concept-phase.md)).

The challenger does not write the answers and does not propose the concept. It may name published games, clichés, and
risks, with sources. It judges only the words of the human: not the suggestions of the coordinator, and not its own
research.

## The three verdicts

- **Accepted:** the words of the human meet the pass condition.
- **Refused:** the human gave a direct answer, and the answer does not meet the pass condition. The reason says what to
  change.
- **No answer yet:** the human did not answer this question. When the words of the human touch the question without an
  answer (for example three possible hooks, and no choice), the challenger writes a **pre-check**: what its research
  says about these candidates. A pre-check is not an answer for the human.

## The parts

| No. | Part | The question | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The sentence | Say the game in one sentence: who, does what most of the time, feels what. | One sentence; a stranger can repeat it. |
| 2 | The nearest games | Which five published games are the nearest to the whole concept? What does each one do? Which ones did you play or watch? | Five real games; the human played or watched the nearest two or three, and shows it (notes, a list). Not only famous names. |
| 3 | The visible difference | What does a player see in a screenshot or in the first five minutes that is not in those games? | A difference in the play or the presentation, not only "better", "bigger", "more content", "realistic", or "made with AI". |
| 4 | The first ideas | First say the theme in one phrase. Then: what are the five most obvious games (or ideas) for that theme? Why is this concept none of them? | The human wrote the list before reading the list of the challenger; the list is honest; the concept is outside it. |
| 5 | The subtraction test | Which part is the twist? Take away the setting: is the game still different? Take away the twist: is it still different? | The human names the twist; at least one of the two tests still gives a different game. |
| 6 | The personal angle | Why are you the person to make this? What do you bring that a prompt does not bring? Where does a player see it? | A concrete knowledge, place, experience, or taste that shows in the game. |
| 7 | The core verb | What does the player do most of the time, in one verb? Is it fun for ten minutes without art? How will you test that? | One verb, and a cheap prototype plan for it with a date. |
| 8 | The hook | In ten words or less, why does a player stop and look? | The hook is a part of the play, not only of the theme. When the hook is an AI feature: a blind test (the AI against a scripted version) is in the prototype plan. |
| 9 | The scope | What is the smallest version that still gives the fantasy? Who makes it, in how many weeks? What do you cut first? | A first playable that the team can make in the planned weeks; a cut list in order. |
| 10a | AI in production | Which parts do AI sessions make? Which decisions stay human? What do players see that AI made? How do you disclose it in the store? | Each visible part has a human decision; the store disclosure is planned. |
| 10b | Live AI in the play | Does the game run an AI while it plays (characters, rulers, text, voices)? If yes: what can the AI change, and what can it not change? Which person writes its rules and its voice? Which guardrails and which red-team test? What does it cost for each player? Does it use brand names? | No live AI, or: limits that a human set; a red-team test in the prototype plan; a cost for each player that the business model pays; brand names only with the permission of their owners. |
| 11 | Players and money | Online or offline? How many players must play in one world at the same time for the game to work? How does the game earn its cost to run (price, subscription, free to play)? | The game works with the number of players that a new game can really get at the start; the business model pays the cost to run for each player (servers, AI calls). |
