# The originality gate: challenge record for <title> (<date>)

The challenger session asks each question. The human answers in the human's own words. The challenger writes
"accepted" or "refused", with a reason. A refused part goes back to the human. The concept gate needs ten accepted
parts ([docs/concept-phase.md](../docs/concept-phase.md)).

The challenger does not write the answers and does not propose the concept. It may name published games, clichés, and
risks.

| No. | Part | The question | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The sentence | Say the game in one sentence: who, does what, feels what. | One sentence; a stranger can repeat it. |
| 2 | The nearest games | Which five published games are the nearest? What does each one do? | Five real games, played or watched by the human; not only famous names. |
| 3 | The visible difference | What does a player see in a screenshot or in the first five minutes that is not in those games? | A difference in the play or the presentation, not only "better", "bigger", "more content", or "made with AI". |
| 4 | The first ideas | What are the five most obvious games for this theme? Why is this concept none of them? | The list is honest; the concept is outside it. |
| 5 | The subtraction test | Take away the setting: is the game still different? Take away the twist of the mechanics: is it still different? | At least one of the two still gives a different game. |
| 6 | The personal angle | Why are you the person to make this? What do you bring that a prompt does not bring? | A concrete knowledge, place, experience, or taste that shows in the game. |
| 7 | The core verb | What does the player do most of the time? Is it fun for ten minutes without art? How will you test that? | One verb, and a cheap prototype plan for it. |
| 8 | The hook | In ten words or less, why does a player stop and look? | The hook is a part of the play, not only of the theme. |
| 9 | The scope | What is the smallest version that still gives the fantasy? What do you cut first? | A first playable that the team can make in the planned weeks; a cut list. |
| 10 | The AI use | Which parts do AI sessions make? Which decisions stay human? What do players see that AI made? | Each visible part has a human decision; the store disclosure is planned. |
